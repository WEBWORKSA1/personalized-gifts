What to write on a memorial gift

Gentle, practical guidance on names, dates and wording for remembrance gifts, with examples.

By the Personalized.Gifts editors. Updated November 19, 2026. How we research

In short

  • Name and dates are complete on their own.
  • Describe the person in plain words. Avoid explaining the loss.
  • There is no deadline. Later is often kinder.

People put off memorial gifts because they are afraid of saying the wrong thing. The fear is reasonable, and the solution is simple: say less. The family will read these words many times. Plain ones hold up.

Start with the facts

Take the full name and both dates from the funeral notice or order of service. Do not rely on memory for a middle name or a year of birth. If the family used a familiar name, such as Nan, Papa or Bill, use that for anything given to the family, and the formal name for anything public such as a bench.

If you add a line

Describe who they were or what they loved. Short, concrete and in the present tense where you can.

  • "Loved this garden."
  • "First to arrive, last to leave."
  • "Sit a while."
  • "Always had time."
  • "The best of dogs."

Lines to avoid: anything that explains or softens the death, anything that tells the family how to feel, and religious wording unless you know it matches their belief.

Their own handwriting

A signature from a birthday card or a line from a recipe can be copied onto jewellery, a board or a print. Ask before using something private. Photograph it flat, in daylight, without shadow, and send the maker the largest file you have.

Choosing the object

If you are close family, something worn or kept near: a pendant, a key ring, a frame. If you are a friend or colleague, something that asks nothing of them: a marker for the garden, a candle, a tree planted in their name with a card. Avoid anything large that the family must find room for.

For a pet

Grief for an animal is real grief. A frame with a photograph and the collar tag, with the name and years, is enough. A line about what they did best, such as "Chief of the sofa", lets the owner smile.

Timing

There is no need to hurry. The first weeks bring a flood of flowers and cards. A small, considered gift that arrives a month or two later, or on the first birthday or anniversary without them, tells the family that the person is still remembered when everyone else has gone quiet.

When a letter is better

If you are not sure a personalized gift is welcome, write a letter instead. Share one specific memory. It costs nothing, it cannot be the wrong size, and it is very often the thing people keep.

Questions people ask

Should I include the dates of birth and death?

On anything formal or outdoor, yes. On jewellery or a private keepsake, the name or a single date is often enough.

Is it appropriate to give a memorial gift to a colleague?

A small one, yes: a card signed by the team and a plant or a donation in the person’s name.

How do I word a gift after a miscarriage or the loss of a baby?

Follow the parents’ lead. If they named the baby, use the name. Keep it very simple, and consider a letter first.
