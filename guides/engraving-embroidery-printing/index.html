Engraving, embroidery or print: which lasts, and which suits what

A plain comparison of the ways gifts are personalized, what each costs, and how each one wears.

By the Personalized.Gifts editors. Updated November 19, 2026. How we research

In short

  • Engraving and embroidery become part of the object; print sits on top of it.
  • Match the method to the material: metal and wood engrave, fabric stitches, ceramic and paper print.
  • Ask which method a listing really uses. “Engraved” sometimes means a surface mark on a coating.

The word personalized covers at least six different processes. They look different, cost different amounts and survive differently, and listings do not always say which one you are buying.

The methods compared

Stamping and embossingLeather, soft metal, paperPermanent; softens with ageInitials
EmbroideryTowels, robes, bags, knitwearOutlasts the fabricNames and monograms

Direct print (UV or ink)Phone cases, wood, cottonGood; can scratch or fadeColour artwork
Vinyl and transfersGlass, tumblers, shirtsFair; edges can liftOne-off craft projects

Engraving

A cutter or laser removes material, so the mark is as durable as the object. On bare metal and wood it is permanent. On coated items, such as powder-coated bottles, the laser removes the coating to show the metal beneath; that is durable too, but it is a different look. Colour is limited to the material itself.

Stamping and embossing

A heated die presses letters into leather, with or without metallic foil. Blind stamping, without foil, darkens and softens as the leather ages. Foil is brighter on day one and can wear on corners that rub.

Embroidery

Thread is stitched through the fabric by machine. It gives height and texture, and it survives hot washes. It needs space: letters under about 6 mm tall close up, so long names go smaller and blur. Thread colour is the main design decision.

Printing

Sublimation turns ink to gas and fixes it into a polymer coating or polyester fibre, which is why a good photo mug survives a dishwasher. Direct printing lays ink on the surface and is the usual method for phone cases and wooden signs. Both handle photographs, which no cutting method can.

Choosing

  • For an heirloom, choose a method that removes or adds material: engraving, stamping, embroidery.
  • For a photograph, choose print, and check the file size first.
  • For something washed weekly, choose embroidery or sublimation.
  • For a one-off made at home, vinyl is quick and forgiving.

Questions people ask

Is laser engraving as good as hand engraving?

For lettering on gifts, yes. Laser work is precise and consistent. Hand engraving has more character and costs more.

Will a printed mug fade?

A sublimation-printed mug should last years of dishwashing. Cheap surface prints fade sooner, so ask how it is made.

What is the cheapest way to personalize a gift?

Print something yourself, or add a hand-written tag. After that, simple engraving on a small item.
