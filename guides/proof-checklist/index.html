Check it before it is permanent: a twelve-point proof

The checks to run on spelling, dates, sizes and photos before you approve a personalized order.

By the Personalized.Gifts editors. Updated November 19, 2026. How we research

In short

  • Most personalized items cannot be returned unless the maker made the error, so the proof is your only safety net.
  • Read names letter by letter and dates digit by digit, against a written source.
  • Save a copy of exactly what you approved.

When you approve a proof you take responsibility for everything on it. Makers reproduce what you send, spelling mistakes included, and personalized goods are generally exempt from the usual right to return something you have changed your mind about. Five minutes here saves the gift.

Names

  1. Spell it from a written source. An email signature, a birth announcement, an invitation. Not memory.
  2. Check capitals and marks. McKenna, van der Berg, Zoë, Renée. Ask whether the font includes accented letters.
  3. Use the name they use. Katherine may be Kate to everyone, and prefer it.
  4. Confirm the order of initials. First, LAST, middle for a centred monogram; first, middle, last for a block one.

Dates and numbers

  1. Check each digit against a calendar. The year is where mistakes hide.
  2. Agree the format. 03.04.2027 is March in one country and April in another. Roman numerals or a written month remove the doubt.
  3. Verify coordinates. Paste them into a map and make sure the pin lands where you expect.

The text itself

  1. Read it aloud, then backwards. Reading word by word in reverse order stops your eye from skipping.
  2. Remove instructions from the text box. "No full stop please" has been engraved on more than one ring.
  3. Check apostrophes. The Rossis, plural, takes none. Rossi's kitchen takes one.

The object

  1. Confirm size, colour and metal. Ring size, necklace length, shirt size, left- or right-handed mug.
  2. Check the photo. Is it sharp at the size you are printing, and will the crop cut anyone off?

After you approve

Take a screenshot of the approved proof and keep the order confirmation. If the finished piece differs from what you approved, the maker should put it right, and those two documents are your evidence. If it matches what you approved, the mistake is yours to live with, which is the whole reason for the list above.

Questions people ask

Can I return a personalized gift?

Usually only if it is faulty or differs from the proof you approved. Check the seller’s policy before you order.

What if the maker does not send a proof?

Ask for one. If none is offered, read your own text again in the order confirmation the moment it arrives and reply at once if anything is wrong.

How quickly can I change an order?

Often only within an hour or two, before production starts. Message the maker directly as soon as you spot a problem.
