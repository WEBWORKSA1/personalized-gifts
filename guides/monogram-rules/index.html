Monogram rules: whose initial goes where

The order of initials for one person, for couples, and for households, with the modern exceptions.

By the Personalized.Gifts editors. Updated November 19, 2026. How we research

In short

  • Centre letter larger: first, LAST, middle. All letters the same size: first, middle, last.
  • For a couple who share a surname, the surname initial goes in the centre with a first initial each side.
  • When in doubt, one letter is always correct.

A monogram is initials arranged as a design. The arrangement follows a convention that is easy to learn and, these days, easy to adapt. What matters is that the person recognises themselves in it.

One person, three initials

There are two layouts. In the classic layout the surname initial stands larger in the centre, with the first-name initial on its left and the middle-name initial on its right. Ava Marie Rossi becomes A, large R, M. In the block layout all three letters are the same size and appear in reading order: A, M, R.

The classic layout is the one most people picture when they hear the word monogram. Use it for script and decorative lettering. Use the block layout for plain capitals on leather goods, luggage and anything sporty.

One person, two initials

No middle name, or prefer not to use it? Two letters of equal size, first then last. Do not enlarge one of two letters; it looks like a mistake.

A single initial

Use the surname initial on things a household shares, such as towels, glasses and a doormat. Use the first-name initial on personal things: a necklace, a notebook, a wash bag. A single letter is also the safest gift when a surname may be about to change.

Couples

For a couple who share a surname, the shared initial sits larger in the centre with one partner's first initial on each side. Older etiquette put the bride's initial on the left. Today the order is the couple's choice, and many simply use the order in which they say their names.

For a couple with different surnames, use the two first initials joined by an ampersand or a small dot, or stack the two surnames' initials. Do not invent a joint surname for them.

One traditional point still worth observing: a joint monogram is used after the wedding. For gifts given before the day, use first names or a date.

Changing names

If someone takes a new surname, the old surname often moves to the middle position. Ask which they prefer. If you cannot ask, use the first initial alone.

Letters that do not behave

Names with prefixes such as Mc, O' or De are usually reduced to the first letter of the surname as the family writes it. Double-barrelled surnames can take both initials in the centre, though four letters crowd a small space. Some script capitals are hard to tell apart, I and J in particular, so check the proof with the font you have chosen.

Questions people ask

Is the middle initial on the left or the right?

On the right, when the surname is larger in the centre. In a block monogram it sits in the middle, in reading order.

Are there different rules for men and women?

Convention once gave the centred style to women and the in-order style to men. Either is now used by anyone, so choose by the look of the lettering.

What do we use if we have different surnames?

Two first initials with an ampersand. It is accurate, balanced, and needs no explanation.
