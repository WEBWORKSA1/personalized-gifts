Personalizing for children: names, spelling and safety

How to personalize gifts for babies and children safely, with spelling checks and age-by-age ideas.

By the Personalized.Gifts editors. Updated November 19, 2026. How we research

In short

  • Get the spelling from a parent in writing, capitals and all.
  • Keep full names off anything worn or carried in public; use initials outside and a label inside.
  • For under-threes, avoid small detachable parts and keep stitching away from where they chew.

Children love seeing their own name, and parents love anything that stops belongings going missing. A few checks make sure the gift is right and safe.

Spelling

Modern names have many spellings. Aiden, Aidan, Ayden and Aaden are four different children. Ask a parent to type the name, and copy it exactly, including any capital in the middle and any accent. For twins and siblings, check each one; they notice.

Names in public

Child-safety advice commonly warns against a visible full name on coats, bags and bottles, because a stranger who can read a name can use it. The practical answer:

  • Outside: initials, a first initial and symbol, or nothing.
  • Inside: first name, surname initial and a parent's phone number on a sewn or stuck label.
  • At home: anything goes. Bedroom doors, stools and books are for full names.

Safety by age

AgeGood choicesAvoid
Under 1Blankets, soft toys with stitched names, keepsake printsBeads, buttons, ribbons, anything that detaches
1 to 3Name puzzles, step stools, board books with their nameSmall puzzle letters for children who still mouth things
4 to 7Name labels, lunch bags, stories starring themFull name on outdoor items
8 to 12Sports bags, notebooks, room signs, initial jewelleryBabyish fonts; let them choose
TeensInitials, dates, in-jokes; subtle beats loudPhotos they have not approved

Look for the safety mark that applies in your country on toys, and check the age guidance on the listing. Jewellery is for supervised wear only on small children, and never for sleeping.

Lowercase helps new readers

Children learn to read and write their name with a capital first letter and lowercase after it. A stool or bedroom sign in that form helps; a name in all capitals is harder for a four-year-old to recognise.

Gifts that record the beginning

For a newborn, collect the facts from the parents: full name, date, time, weight, length and place. Add the birth flower and birthstone for the month. These go on a print, a blanket or the lid of a box, and they are the details the child will ask about at ten.

Gifts the child helps to make

Children like giving, too. A printed certificate they decorate, a coupon book they fill in, or tags they sign for grandparents all count as personalized, and are treasured by the people who receive them.

Questions people ask

Is it safe to put my child’s name on their school bag?

Use initials on the outside and a full label inside. That keeps belongings returnable without displaying the name.

What should I engrave on a christening or naming gift?

The child’s full name and the date. Add the place if there is room.

When can a child wear a name necklace?

Follow the maker’s age guidance. For young children, short supervised wear only, and never in bed.
