Make it yourself: five ways to personalize a gift at home

Vinyl cutting, hand stamping, embroidery, photo transfer and printables compared by cost, difficulty and time.

By the Personalized.Gifts editors. Updated November 19, 2026. How we research

In short

  • Printables need only a printer. Every other method needs a tool, and practice on a spare.
  • Cut vinyl is the quickest way to put a name on glass, tumblers and shirts.
  • Make two. The first is where you learn.

Making the gift yourself is the most personal version of all, and the tools have never been easier to use. Each method below can produce something good on a first weekend. Our video page collects current tutorials for all of them.

Printables

Start here. Design on this site, print on card, trim, and present. There is no skill to learn and the result is immediate.

Cut vinyl

A cutting machine cuts your lettering from adhesive vinyl; you peel away the waste, lift the letters with transfer tape and press them onto the object. Use permanent vinyl for glass and tumblers and iron-on vinyl for fabric. Clean the surface with alcohol first, and remember to mirror the design for iron-on.

Hand stamping

Each letter is struck into a metal blank with one firm hammer blow. Letters will sit slightly unevenly, and that is the charm. Tape a guideline across the blank, practise on a spare, and darken the letters afterwards with enamel or a marker wiped back.

Hand embroidery

Draw the name on the fabric with a wash-out pen, stretch it in a hoop, and follow the line in backstitch or stem stitch. Script letters flow well. Wash the pen marks out when you finish and press from the back.

Laser engraving

Desktop lasers engrave wood, slate, leather and coated metal with shop-quality results. They also need ventilation, eye protection and a test on scrap for every new material. Never engrave unknown plastics or anything containing chlorine, such as vinyl, which gives off harmful fumes.

Getting your design onto the object

Build a monogram or lettering in our tools, download the picture, and import it into your cutting or engraving software. Use the SVG download where you can; it scales to any size without blurring.

Giving handmade

Say that you made it. Add a tag with the date. A slightly crooked letter that someone hammered themselves is worth more to most people than a perfect one from a factory.

Questions people ask

What is the easiest personalized gift to make?

A printed and framed monogram or name poem. After that, a name in vinyl on a tumbler.

Do I need an expensive machine?

No. Printables, hand stamping and hand embroidery all cost very little to start.

Can I sell what I make with your designs?

Designs you create with our tools are yours to use, including on things you sell. The fonts carry open licences, listed on our disclaimer page.
