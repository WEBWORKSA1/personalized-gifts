Ordering ten or more: minimums, pricing and timelines for bulk personalized gifts

How bulk and corporate personalized gift orders work: minimum quantities, what drives price, and a planning timeline.

By the Personalized.Gifts editors. Updated November 19, 2026. How we research

In short

  • Published minimums cluster around 10, 25 and 50 units; on-demand services start at one.
  • Individual names cost more than one repeated logo, because every piece is a separate job.
  • Start six to eight weeks ahead. Production alone is commonly quoted at two to six weeks.

Buying personalized gifts for a team, a wedding party or a client list is a different purchase from buying one. Suppliers quote instead of listing a price, the minimum order matters, and the calendar is longer. Knowing how they think gets you a better quote faster.

What suppliers ask first

Every gifting studio we read opens with the same four questions: what is the occasion, how many gifts, what budget per gift, and when do you need them. Have those answers ready and you skip a round of email.

Minimum quantities

Minimums exist because setting up a logo, a box or an engraving file costs the same for one piece as for a hundred. From supplier pages read in November 2026:

Type of orderMinimums we saw published
Print-on-demand itemsFrom 1 unit
Logo on a card, tag or bandFrom about 10 gifts
Custom kitted or fully branded boxesFrom about 25 units
Imprinted drinkware and promotional itemsOften 50 units

Names, logos or both

A logo is one setup repeated. A name is a new setup for every piece, and a new chance of a spelling mistake. Expect individual names to add cost per unit and time for checking. The best-received corporate gifts carry the recipient's name on the object and the company's mark only on the card or box.

What drives the price

  • Quantity. Unit prices fall at volume breaks; ask where the next one is.
  • Method. One-colour engraving is cheaper than full-colour print or embroidery.
  • Setup and artwork. Often a one-off fee; some suppliers include design help.
  • Packaging. Custom boxes, sleeves and inserts can cost as much as the gift.
  • Delivery. One address is simple. Hundreds of home addresses add per-parcel fees and, for warehoused stock, storage charges.

A planning timeline

Weeks before deliveryWhat happens
8Send the brief; collect quotes
7Choose a supplier; supply logo files and the list of names
6Approve proofs and a sample
5 to 2Production
1Delivery and distribution

The list of names

Send names in a spreadsheet, one row per person, exactly as each should appear. Ask every recipient, or their manager, to confirm their own line. Include a column for delivery address only if gifts go to homes, and share addresses only with the supplier that needs them.

Before you send anything to a client

Check gift policies on both sides. Many organisations limit the value of gifts their staff may accept, and tax treatment of business gifts differs by country, so ask your accountant how they are handled where you are.

Questions people ask

What is the minimum order for personalized corporate gifts?

It depends on how they are branded. Published minimums commonly sit at 10, 25 or 50 units, and print-on-demand services accept single items.

Can each gift have a different name?

Yes. Suppliers call it individual personalization. It adds cost and time, and you must supply a checked list of names.
